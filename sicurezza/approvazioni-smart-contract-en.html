Smart contract approvals: what you sign and how to revoke them

Intermediate 📖 10 min read 📅 Updated April 2026

Versione italiana · ← All security guides

Every time you use Uniswap, OpenSea, or a DeFi protocol, you sign approvals allowing a smart contract to move your tokens. Understanding what you sign is essential for security.

What are approvals

An approval (allowance) authorizes a contract to withdraw a certain amount of tokens from your wallet without asking each time.

Types

  • Limited — specific amount (safer)
  • Unlimited — full token access (risky)

Risks of open approvals

If a contract is compromised or was malicious from the start, it can drain approved tokens — even months later.

How to check approvals

  1. Visit revoke.cash or Etherscan Token Approvals
  2. Connect your wallet (only on trusted sites)
  3. Check each network: Ethereum, Polygon, BSC, Arbitrum
  4. Revoke old or suspicious approvals

Best practices

  • Approve only the amount needed for a single operation
  • Use a separate wallet for experimental DeFi
  • Review approvals every 1-3 months
  • Before revoking, check network fees

FAQ

Is revoking an approval dangerous?
No, it is a standard operation. It only costs the network fee for the revoke transaction.
Do I need this on all chains?
Yes. Each network has separate approvals. Check Ethereum, L2s, and sidechains where you have operated.
